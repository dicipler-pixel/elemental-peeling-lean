------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  THE CLAIM IN ONE PAGE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four developments -- the elemental peel and its loop wall, Dirac time, VOTKP, and
Ramanujan's gamma fractions -- are written in one small algebra: a positive ratio z, the
reflection

    Gamma = (z - 1)/(z + 1) = tanh( (1/2) log z ),

one distinguished value Gamma^2 = 1/2, and the rule that reflections compose by adding
their rapidities.  A fifth body, the twin-prime key, shares only the ledger vocabulary, and
the periodic table supplies the data these readings are tested against.  The algebra is old;
what is new here is that part of the sharing is compiled rather than asserted, and that the
part which is not compiled is marked as such.

The paper makes three claims of three different kinds: a theorem-level synthesis, an
algebraic consequence illustrated by archive counts, and an empirical non-selection result.

First, the developments share a coordinate, and three of the interfaces between them are
compiled.  In those three places this is not a family resemblance: the two predictive
quotients are proved to be the same subspace, Dirac time's path-descent theorem is
specialised to the peel ledger, and two peel steps are proved to compose by the velocity
law.  Elsewhere the sharing is a reading -- the continued-fraction faces and the knot
involution are the two clear cases -- and Section 4 says which is which, sentence by
sentence.

Second, the algebra has consequences the individual papers did not draw, and some of them
are negative.  A window of peel steps can cross the wall with every one of its steps
inside, because reflections add; the archive at the stipulated cutoff supplies 91,638 such
windows among its 91,768 crossings.  The theorem explains how each of those crossings is
possible; the count and the percentage are measurements of this archive, not consequences of
addition.  For a window whose steps are all inside the wall, the geometric mean can never
cross -- that one is a theorem about any positive ladder.  The practical point is narrower
than it first looks: thresholding each step into inside or outside does not reveal the
accumulated crossings, although the products of the tabulated ratios do determine them.

Third, and this is the part a reader should weigh hardest: the empirical layer does not
select the constant.  The archive's own numbers leave a band of cutoffs that classify every
step identically, and 3 + 2*sqrt(2) sits inside that band without being picked out by it.
The table is consistent with the wall; it does not measure it.  Any claim that the periodic
table "confirms" the value 5.828427 is not supported by the shipped data, and Section 5.3
gives the cutoff bands the baseline and the baseline-plus-anion data leave unresolved.

Alongside the three claims the paper carries a list of its own corrections.  Twelve
statements transcribed from earlier prose are false as written and are kept as refutation
theorems beside their corrected forms; two structural results are negative -- composition
does not select the ledger readout, and the predictive dimension of a peel ladder is
model-dependent rather than intrinsic.  Section 7 is that list, and it is part of what the
paper offers rather than an appendix to it.

What the body is worth, stated plainly: as new mathematics it is small -- the algebra is
textbook reflection and the finite-dimensional version of the main theorem is standard
observability theory.  As a checked artifact it is real: roughly sixteen hundred named
results in one build, a corrigenda list of statements that turned out false, and a data
layer whose every published count reproduces from the shipped files.  As predictive power
over chemistry it is, so far, nothing that has been demonstrated.  The paper is written to
make that distinction impossible to blur.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  WHAT THIS PAPER IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is a synthesis with a verification standard.  Every exact statement is either checked by
the Lean kernel or reproduced by a named script from shipped data; every other statement is
labelled a measurement or a metaphor.  The three labels are used strictly:

    PROVED     a Lean theorem, compiled, with an axiom audit showing only propext,
               Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
    MEASURED   a finite computation by a named script from versioned shipped inputs -- the
               archive, the source tree, or a database snapshot -- exact as arithmetic
               within its stated scope, and true only of those inputs.  A finite sample does
               not establish a limit, and an unsourced comparison is not a measurement.
    METAPHOR   a reading that organizes the work and is not a mathematical statement --
               "tinder", "kindling", the ledger as a balance sheet, the wall as a
               boundary between two kinds of removal.

It is not a physical theory.  Nothing here derives a Hamiltonian, a force, an optical
constant or an ionisation energy.  The one place where a rate is identified with another
rate (Section 4.2) is an identification inside a model, not a derivation of either rate
from a mechanism.  The repository's LIMITATIONS files say this for each leg and they are
not weakened here.

It is also not a priority claim.  The reflection identity, the Riccati flow it satisfies and
the tangent-half-angle substitution are nineteenth- and twentieth-century material; Section
8 names what I could find and says what I could not.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  THE ALGEBRA
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1  The peel ratio and the reflection.

Take a ladder of positive readings I_1, I_2, ... -- removal energies of one atom, or any
other ordered positive sequence.  A step is the ratio z = I_{k+1}/I_k.  The reflection is

    Gamma = (z - 1)/(z + 1).

Three facts, all elementary, are the whole of the coordinate:

    Gamma = tanh( (1/2) log z )          the same number in hyperbolic form (DLMF 4.37.24)
    z = (1 + Gamma)/(1 - Gamma)          the inverse, a Mobius map
    Gamma(1/z) = -Gamma(z)               reversal flips the sign, keeps the square

The third is why every readout built from Gamma^2 is blind to reversal.  That sounds like a
technicality and turns out to be the organizing fact of Section 7.

    PROVED: LoopWall/Ledger.lean, gam_eq_tanh_half_log, gam_reverse, gam_sq_reverse;
            unified/VOTKP/Smith.lean, smith_identity (an independent second proof of the
            same identity); unified/Bridge/Reflection.lean, two_smith_identities re-expresses
            that proof using LoopWall's definition of gam.  The LoopWall proof proper is
            LoopWall/Ledger.lean, gam_eq_tanh_half_log.  Both remain in the tree; nothing is
            deduplicated, and the word "collapsed" would be wrong.

3.2  The wall.

One value is distinguished: Gamma^2 = 1/2.  Over the positive ratios it is exactly

    z = 3 + 2*sqrt(2) = 5.828427...   or   z = 3 - 2*sqrt(2) = 0.171573...,

the two roots being reciprocal.  The value 3 + 2*sqrt(2) is (1 + sqrt(2))^2, so its
logarithm is twice log(1 + sqrt(2)); in ledger units the wall is the quantised value
log z = +/- 2 log(1 + sqrt(2)).

    PROVED: LoopWall/Wall.lean, wall_eq_iff, wall_ratios_reciprocal;
            unified/Bridge/Reflection.lean, wall_ledger_value.

Why call it a wall?  Because the disk has two halves and the number 1/2 is where the two
shares are equal: in the lossless two-channel normalisation the reflected and transmitted
shares are Gamma^2 and 1 - Gamma^2, and at Gamma^2 = 1/2 they coincide.  Nothing here fixes
whether Gamma is an amplitude or a power; if it were an amplitude reflection coefficient the
returned power fraction would be Gamma^2 and equal shares at the wall would still hold, with
|Gamma| = 1/sqrt(2).  The name is a definition, not a discovery; what makes the value carry
weight is that it keeps appearing where a ladder changes the qualitative behaviour of a sum.

3.3  The ledger, and why steps add.

The rapidity r = log z = 2 artanh(Gamma) is additive under composition of steps: a window of
steps has ratio the product of its ratios, so its rapidity is the sum.  In the reflection
coordinate this is the velocity-addition law

    Gamma(z1 z2) = Gamma(z1) (+) Gamma(z2),    u (+) v = (u + v)/(1 + uv),

which is the addition law of relativistic velocities written in the tangent-half-angle
variable.  So peel steps add like velocities, not like ratios.

Two consequences, and they are the mathematical core of this paper:

    (a) A window exceeds the upper wall 3 + 2*sqrt(2) exactly when the sum of its steps'
        rapidities exceeds log(3 + 2*sqrt(2)).  No single step need cross.  (The lower
        branch 3 - 2*sqrt(2) is the same statement after a sign change.)
    (b) The geometric mean of steps that are each inside the wall is inside the wall --
        always, for any positive ladder, because a mean is bounded by its largest term.

Together they say that crossing is a property of the sum and not of the terms, and that the
common intuition "an average of inside things is inside" is true while the corresponding
intuition about products is false.

    PROVED: unified/Bridge/Composition.lean -- log_prod_eq_sum, composite_reflection_eq,
            composite_two_eq_oplus, crosses_iff_ledger_sum, window_at_wall_ledger,
            two_halves_cross (two steps at z = 5/2 are each inside; their product 25/4 is
            outside), geomean_lt_wall, composition_crosses_mean_does_not.

3.4  What is definitional here.

Worth saying before the applications: z, Gamma, the wall value and the composition law are
definitions plus algebra.  Nothing in Section 3 is a discovery, and a paper that presented
them as one would deserve the scepticism it got.  Their role is to be the common coordinate
in which four developments and a data layer become comparable, and to make certain claims provable
that had been asserted in prose.

4.  FOUR INSTANTIATIONS, A COUSIN, AND A DATA LAYER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  Elemental peeling.

Read an element as the ladder of its successive ionisation energies and each adjacent ratio
as a step.  The shipped archive has 3,570 steps from hydrogen to astatine; eight of them lie
outside the wall, and every one of the eight is the first opening of a noble-gas core
(Li k=1, Be k=2, B k=3, C k=4 into helium; Na k=1 into neon; K k=1 into argon; Rb k=1 into
krypton; Cs k=1 into xenon).  A further 65 "anion rungs" -- the ratio from the electron
affinity to the first ionisation energy -- give 53 more values outside.

    MEASURED: scripts/atlas_census.py; core rule k = Z - N verified 305/305 against the
    archive's own builder.

The reading is that the first opening of a core is a large step and the rest of the ladder
is a sequence of small ones -- a "wall" between the inner shells and the valence ladder.  As
a description this is exact arithmetic.  As an explanation it is a restatement: the eight
steps are large because a core opening costs much more energy than the removal that follows,
which is what "core opening" means.

4.2  Dirac time.

The predictive quotient of a linear system -- the state space modulo everything no future
reading can see -- is Section 2 of "The Dirac Time of the Gigantefermion".  It is the
finite-dimensional observability quotient of linear systems theory: N is the intersection of
the kernels of R L^n, the quotient is V/N, and its dimension is the rank of the
observability map.  The Dirac-time paper proves it, VOTKP proves it again as Theorem 3.1,
and the two versions are the same subspace (unified/Bridge/Quotient.lean,
unseen_eq_unobservable) -- the bridge being Cayley-Hamilton, which is what collapses the
infinite intersection to a finite one.

The same paper's common-clock theorem gives, for a generator with eigen-data, the gap law

    mu_j - mu_k = tau_dot (E_j - E_k).

Read as a peel rate this says omega = tau_dot (E_j - E_k): the frequency of the peel flow is
the clock rate times the energy gap.  For a real gap delta and the zero-offset flow
Gamma(tau) = tanh(delta tau / 2), the squared wall has exactly one forward solution with
tau >= 0 precisely when delta is nonzero, namely

    |delta| * tau_wall = 2 artanh(1/sqrt(2)) = log(3 + 2*sqrt(2)),

and a zero gap has none.  Three hypotheses are doing work and are stated here rather than
left in the Lean: the initial offset is zero, the gap is real, and it is compared against
the squared wall.  With a nonzero offset the assertion is false -- Gamma(tau) = tanh(1 +
tau/2) starts above 1/sqrt(2) and never returns.

    PROVED: the gap law itself is unified/DiracTime/CommonClock.lean, common_gap_scaling;
    its projection to a real gap is Found/CommonClock.lean, real_common_clock_gap; the peel
    substitution is gapPeel_of_common_gap, and then hasDerivAt_gapPeel, gam_gapRatio,
    gapPeel_eq_wall_iff, existsUnique_forward_wall_iff, forward_wall_time_mul_abs_gap.

This is the closest join in the body and the one most easily over-read.  The identification
is exact inside the model; it does not say that any physical peel has this rate, and the
paper does not claim it.

4.3  VOTKP.

The same coordinate with the composition law made the centre of the theory: the Smith
coordinate, the velocity addition law, the film echo, the exact circle that decides whether
a thin film raises or lowers the returned power.  The predictive quotient appears here too.
Of the four, VOTKP is the one whose own theorem -- that peel steps add like velocities -- is
the composition law of Section 3.3 stated for the peel.

4.4  Ramanujan's gamma fractions.

The gamma quotient is

    4 Gamma((x+3)/4)^2 / Gamma((x+1)/4)^2 = x + 1^2/(2x + 3^2/(2x + 5^2/(2x + ...))).

Its n-th term, as a transfer matrix, is [[0, (2n-1)^2], [1, 2x]], with characteristic roots
x +- s where s = sqrt(x^2 + (2n-1)^2) -- a derivation in the session record, and *not*
formalised in this tree.  What the tree does prove is the algebra of the face that
derivation names.  Define

    z_n = (s + x)/(s - x),    Gamma_n = x / sqrt(x^2 + (2n-1)^2);

then Gamma_n = (z_n - 1)/(z_n + 1) exactly, and the wall Gamma_n^2 = 1/2 is exactly
2n - 1 = x.  The identification of these z_n and Gamma_n with the fraction's own terms is a
reading, not a theorem here; the identities are about the definitions.  Granting the
reading, this is the peel coordinate again, with the fraction's parameter x in the role of
the charge, and:

    for positive real x and n >= 1, a face is on the wall exactly when 2n - 1 = x; since
    2n - 1 runs over the positive odd integers, an odd x has a wall face and an even x does
    not (arithmetic on the theorem, not a separate declaration).
    Brouncker's fraction (x = 1) has it at the first face, with z_1 = 3 + 2*sqrt(2).
    x = 3 has it at the second face; x = 5 at the third; and so on.

    PROVED (about the definitions above): unified/Bridge/Faces.lean -- faceRatio_eq_gam,
    faceGamma_sq_eq_half_iff, faceGamma_sq_eq_half_iff_odd, faceGamma_lt_one,
    brouncker_first_face.
    NOT FORMALISED: that the continued fraction's terms are these faces, its convergents
    are products of these matrices, or that any convergent equals a product of the z_n.

The session record reports that the fraction's truncation error falls like N^(-x), that
|error| N^x tends to 4/pi^2 at x = 1 and to 1/4 at x = 3, and that error times the product of
the face ratios stays nearly fixed.  Neither constant is derived there or here.  What this
repository reproduces is the finite evidence, and only that: at N = 6400 the script gives
0.4053049 and 0.24999999 against 4/pi^2 = 0.4052847 and 1/4 -- agreement to five figures,
which is not a proof of a limit, and the script's tolerance is not one either.  The
attenuation rule does survive its own test: error times the face product varies by 0.3 per
cent at x = 1 and at x = 3 while the error falls by four orders of magnitude, and by 5 per
cent at x = 1/2.  The power law itself is *motivated* by Section 3.3: the sum of rapidities
grows like x log N, so the product of the z's grows like N^x, and one expects the error to
fall like N^(-x).  The step from a product of faces to the truncation error of a
non-commuting stack is not an implication, and no theorem here derives it.

    MEASURED (finite, at the stated N): scripts/ramanujan_faces.py -- the values above, the
    attenuation spreads, and the angle table, which the session's own checker also gives.
    OPEN: the two limits, the N^(-x) law, and the derivation of either constant.

4.5  The twin-prime key.

The same ledger language, applied to gaps between primes rather than energies: end digits,
the two ladders p = 1 and p = 5 mod 6, twin starts, handoff air, merged quadruplets.  The
arithmetic part is proved -- every prime above 3 lies on exactly one ladder, every twin
start above 3 is on the 5-ladder, every twin start above 5 is 11, 17 or 29 mod 30, every
handoff air is 4 mod 6, a four-prime merge can only start at 11 mod 30.

    PROVED: unified/PrimeGap/ (copied byte-identically from results/), 37 declarations.

The counts -- 50,847,534 primes below 10^9, 27,412,679 twin pairs below 10^10, the
transition matrices and their decay -- are measurements and are kept apart from the
arithmetic in results/.  This leg contributes no peel statement beyond the ledger form; it
is in the paper because it is the same author's ledger used on a different object, and
because pretending otherwise would be the kind of unification this paper is trying to avoid.

5.  THE DATA LAYER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1  What reproduces.

The shipped archive has 118 elements and 5,714 removal steps, of which the hydrogen-to-
astatine baseline is 85 elements and 3,570 steps.  Every published count in the periodic
table and the scale atlas reproduces from the shipped JSON by one script:

    3,570 steps, 8 outside the wall, all eight first core openings, all at k <= 4
    65 anion rungs, 53 outside
    83 of 83 elements Li-At whose largest step opens a core (78 helium, 2 neon, 1 each
        argon, krypton, xenon)
    5,714 steps in the full archive, the same eight outliers
    183,770 contiguous windows of at least two steps
    91,768 of them above the wall in total
    91,638 of those with every constituent step inside
    0 above the wall on geometric mean per step
    largest two-step mean 4.76566745 (Li), largest three-step mean 3.15005083 (B)
    183,081 windows with both endpoint widths usable, 689 without
    0 usable windows above the wall on the upper endpoint mean

    MEASURED: scripts/atlas_census.py, which reproduces the atlas's own computation --
    z = ie[j]/ie[i] over the two endpoint thresholds, consecutive charge labels, and the
    per-element uncertainty list for the endpoint rectangle.

The rule that makes the window count exact is stated in the atlas's prose and easy to miss:
every intervening charge must be present, so a window is a run of consecutive k and not a
slice of the step list.  Dropping only that requirement, with ratios still taken from the
endpoint energies, gives 191,817 / 98,572 / 98,442; dropping it and also switching to
products of the rounded stored step ratios gives 191,817 / 95,569 / 95,439.  The main triple
is the same under both ratio conventions, which is the reassuring part: it is the
consecutive-charge rule, not the choice of ratio convention, that does the work.  This is
worth stating because it is the kind of rule a later reader re-deriving the numbers will
otherwise get wrong, as this one did.

5.2  The composition finding.

Of the 91,768 windows that exceed the wall, 91,638 do so with every constituent step inside
it: 99.86 per cent.  The composition theorem explains how each of those is possible; it does
not imply the number, which depends on this archive's window lengths and ratio distribution.
That distinction is the honest one -- addition makes accumulated crossings possible, and
this archive is full of them.  The remaining 130 windows contain an outside step and cross
trivially.  A summary that keeps only each step's inside/outside label cannot recover the
other 91,638; the products of the tabulated ratios can.

5.3  What the table cannot select.

Order the 3,570 baseline step ratios.  The largest inside the wall is 5.639665 (nitrogen,
k=5); the smallest outside is 5.947101 (caesium, k=1).  Every cutoff in the interval
between them classifies all 3,570 steps identically, and 3 + 2*sqrt(2) = 5.828427 lies
inside that interval.  Adding the 65 anion rungs narrows the interval to
[5.807902, 5.866539) -- silver's rung is the largest inside, silicon's the smallest outside
-- which still contains the wall.

So the archive is consistent with the wall and cannot distinguish it, at this resolution,
from any value in a band of about one per cent around it.  That is the honest statement and
it is the opposite of a confirmation.

Nor do the aggregate counts rescue it.  The 91,768 and 91,638 are deterministic functions
of the cutoff: at c = 5.85, inside the band, the per-step classification is unchanged --
still eight outside, all core openings -- but the crossing count falls to 91,616 and the
accumulated-only count to 91,486.  The point is not that a computed quantity is never
evidence; it is that these counts have no independently measured target in the archive, so
reproducing their published values cannot select the cutoff.  The raw endpoint ratios are
cutoff-independent; which compositions count as accumulated-only is not.

    MEASURED: scripts/atlas_census.py computes the ordinary-step band, the combined band
    from the 65 anion ratios, and both censuses at c = 5.85; scripts/check_paper_numbers.py
    checks the c = 5.85 count against this text.

5.4  The shapes.

The table also reports that reading each element's first eight steps on other shapes --
triangle, square, diamond, hexagon -- groups elements by column far better than the raw
energies do.  On the archive's 77-element comparison (scripts/shapes_recall.py) the reported
scores are circle 53 of 77, diamond 52, cube 48, simplex 60 and raw energies 4; simplex is
numerically the highest of the four shape scores on that comparison.  The scoring target and the evaluation
population are fixed by that comparison, and without them the numbers say nothing more.

The same sentence adds that no shape beats the circle by more than its bootstrap spread.
That part cannot be checked: no resampling unit, replicate count, seed or spread is shipped,
and the observed simplex-minus-circle gap is 7 of 77.  The paper reports the recomputable
half and says the other half is unreproducible.

    MEASURED: scripts/shapes_recall.py -- the four scores, both raw-energy baselines and the
    uniform chance level.  NOT REPRODUCIBLE: the bootstrap clause.

6.  CHIRALITY, AND WHAT THE WALL CANNOT SEE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reversal sends z to 1/z and Gamma to -Gamma.  So any readout that factors through Gamma^2
is invariant under reversal: it cannot tell a ratio from its reciprocal, and it cannot tell
the two branches of the wall apart.  Two statements, carefully separated:

    a single ratio that is fixed by reversal has z = 1 and Gamma = 0, and so is at neither
    branch of the wall;

    a ladder that is fixed by reversal as a sequence -- palindromic readings -- does *not*
    have Gamma = 0 at every step.  The readings 1, W, 1 with W = 3 + 2*sqrt(2) are unchanged
    by reversal, and both of their steps are exactly at the wall.  Reversal reorders and
    reciprocates the steps; it does not by itself keep a ladder off the wall.

The first is a statement about one number and is proved (`mirror_fixed_iff_gam_eq_zero`).
The second is the reason the distinction has to be made in words: conflating them is an
error this draft made in its first version.

What the wall does hide is the sign.  Each branch has nonzero Gamma, and any quantity built
from Gamma^2 reports the same number at +Gamma and -Gamma.  That is blindness to the sign of
one coordinate, and it is all that is proved; the signed quantity log z and Gamma itself are
computed throughout the papers and are of course reversal-sensitive.  No further notion of
chirality is formalised here.

The same elementary argument appears twice more in the body.  In the Dirac-time paper a
chiral pairing S T S^-1 = -T pairs the spectrum under mu -> -mu and kills every odd spectral
moment; in the knot work theta(mirror K) = -theta(K), so an amphichiral knot has theta = 0.
The argument in all three places is: an invariant that is odd under an involution vanishes
on the fixed locus.

It is an analogy among proofs, not a compiled three-way interface, and the tree must not be
read as saying otherwise.  What is proved is the generic lemma, the LoopWall reversal
identity, and an abstract theta schema in which `K`, `mirror` and `theta` are arbitrary with
oddness and fixedness as hypotheses.  The Dirac spectral theorem is a separate argument, not
an instance of the generic lemma in this tree, and no knot invariant is formalised.

    PROVED: unified/Bridge/Chirality.lean -- odd_involution_vanishes (generic),
    theta_vanishes_on_amphichiral (the schema), even_readout_blind, gam_mirror,
    mirror_fixed_iff_gam_eq_zero, wall_is_chiral (which states only that the wall ratio has
    nonzero Gamma and is not 1).
    NOT COMPILED AS INSTANCES: the Dirac chiral pairing, and any actual knot.

The lemma gives one direction only.  It does not say that theta = 0 forces a knot to be
amphichiral, and the paper is careful about this because the knot census invites exactly
that over-read: over all 12,965 prime knots through 13 crossings, KnotInfo release 2026.11.2 has 78
amphicheiral -- 47 negative, 30 fully, 1 positive -- and the set is name-for-name identical
to the 78 with theta = 0.  Both lists ship: the KnotInfo snapshot in
knots/data/knotinfo_amphichiral_2026.11.2.csv and the VOTKP census in
knots/data/census_through_13.csv, compared by scripts/knot_census.py.  There is no exception in either
direction, and zero amphicheiral knots at 11 or at 13 crossings.

That is a complete finite verification and it is still not a proof of the converse: the
converse is a statement about all knots, and the census stops at 13 crossings.  A single odd
function that vanishes away from its fixed locus -- x^3 - x at x = 1 under x -> -x -- shows
that oddness alone can never yield the converse.

    MEASURED: scripts/knot_census.py, over the two shipped lists (KnotInfo 2026.11.2).
    PROVED: odd_does_not_imply_converse.
    OPEN: the converse (the knot paper's Conjecture 21).

7.  THE NEGATIVE RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Half of what this body establishes is that certain attractive statements are false or
undecidable from the data.  They are collected here because they are the part least likely
to be rediscovered and most likely to be re-asserted.

    7.1  Composition does not select the ledger readout.  Evenness, vanishing at zero
         balance and append-additivity of a channel sum leave both the log ledger and the
         Gamma^2 readout admissible.  Selection needs additivity of a determinant readout
         plus regularity.  (LoopWall: append_additivity_alone_does_not_select_ledger,
         CompositionBridge.eq_const_mul_log_of_append_additive.)

    7.2  Twelve statements transcribed from the papers' prose are false as written and are
         kept as refutation theorems beside their corrected forms, in code and in
         LIMITATIONS.md and COVERAGE.md.  They include the sign of the Euler-Lagrange
         term, the wall-clock factor, the sign of the readout coefficient, the claim that
         the square of a scale bound suffices, and the norm-one quaternion count in Z^4.

    7.3  The predictive quotient of a peel ladder is one-dimensional in the scalar model,
         so the wall is not a memory transition there -- but this is model-dependent and
         not intrinsic.  Other linearisations of the same ladder do separate the wall, and
         one changes dimension at z = 1 instead.  (unified/Found/PredictivePeel.lean.)

    7.4  The block determinant identity is not what it looks like: 1 - Gamma^2 = 4 det p
         holds for one two-channel block; the blockDet of the composition bridge is a
         product over blocks and equals (1 - Gamma^2)^n.  Two wall blocks give 1/4, not
         1/2.  (unified/Found/WallReadout.lean.)

    7.5  Perfect reflection is unattained.  det p = 0 corresponds to |Gamma| = 1, and for a
         positive peel ratio |Gamma| < 1 strictly, so the block determinant never vanishes.

These are not embarrassments to be trimmed from a submission, and they are not evidence for
the positive statements either.  Their content is that certain attractive sentences are
false or undecidable from the data, which is worth knowing on its own.  The assurance for
each positive statement comes separately, from its cited kernel-checked proof or its
reproducible measurement -- that is the standard of Section 2, and the corrigenda neither
add to it nor substitute for it.

8.  PRIOR ART, AND WHAT IS ACTUALLY NEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Riccati equations for nonuniform transmission lines were standard long before this work.
Pierce gave the impedance Riccati equation and its uniform-line tanh solution in 1943 (Bell
System Technical Journal 22, 263-265, which calls it a Riccati equation on p. 263 and gives
a tanh solution on p. 264); Walker and Wax gave the voltage-reflection form in 1946 (Journal
of Applied Physics 17, 1043-1045).  The particular solution Gamma = tanh(omega t / 2) follows
from the derivative and identity in DLMF 4.34.3 and 4.35.12, and needs no transmission-line
attribution at all.  The identity Gamma = tanh((1/2) log z) is the logarithmic form of
artanh, DLMF 4.37.24: artanh u = (1/2) ln((1+u)/(1-u)).  The addition law is DLMF 4.35.3,
which is also the relativistic velocity law in the tangent-half-angle variable.  The
continued-fraction step matrix [[0, a_n], [1, b_n]] and its convergent recurrence are
standard: DLMF 1.12(ii) for the recurrence, Paydon and Wall (Duke Math. J. 9, 1942) for the
sequence-of-linear-transformations reading, and Kisil (arXiv:1412.1457, section 2) for a
modern exposition.  The finite-dimensional predictive quotient is the observability quotient
of linear systems theory, and the fact that a product exceeds a threshold exactly when the
sum of logarithms does is DLMF 4.8.2 with monotonicity of the logarithm.

None of that is claimed as new, and the earlier version of this section was wrong to name
Walker and Wax as the closest source for the tanh solution: Pierce precedes them, and the
special solution is an exercise in the DLMF identities.

What this paper offers, then, is not an algebra and not a criterion:

    -- a proof-level synthesis of six named works by this author, with the duplicate
       repository proofs kept and their interfaces stated as theorems; this is an
       integration of my own repositories, not a claim to a new algebraic object.  The six,
       with the versions this paper was checked against, are the loop-wall development in
       this repository, "The Dirac Time of the Gigantefermion" (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22978630),
       "VOTKP: The Vector of Traits Kept under Peeling" (v2.9, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23340757),
       the periodic table v2.2, the Ramanujan-faces session record of 13-15 November 2026, and
       the twin-prime key in results/ -- the last of which, as Sections 4.5 and 9 say, shares
       only the ledger vocabulary;
    -- the application to removal-step ladders of the elementary facts that a product
       exceeds a threshold exactly when the sum of logarithms does and that a geometric mean
       is bounded by the largest term, together with the archive counts and their Lean
       formalisation.  No new composition criterion is claimed;
    -- the restatement in peel notation of the eigenvalues of the standard continued-fraction
       step matrix: with r = x/(2n-1), z_n = exp(2 asinh r) and
       Gamma_n = tanh(asinh r) = x/sqrt(x^2 + (2n-1)^2), whence Gamma_n^2 = 1/2 exactly when
       2n - 1 = x.  I have not located this displayed peel formulation elsewhere and claim
       only the formulation and the odd-x observation, not a new matrix construction;
    -- the itemised corrigenda to my earlier statements, and the computation that this
       shipped archive leaves an interval of cutoffs with identical per-step classifications
       while yielding different aggregate window counts inside that interval.  Both are
       repository- and archive-specific, and neither is claimed as a general phenomenon.

The last is the most defensible contribution and the least exciting: a checked statement of
what the evidence does not show.

9.  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    -- no physical mechanism.  No Hamiltonian, force, optical constant or ionisation energy
       is derived.  LIMITATIONS.md for the loop wall, and the corresponding files for Dirac
       time and VOTKP, define the scope and are not widened here.
    -- no predictive power over chemistry.  The body sorts ratios that were already
       measured.  It has not predicted a ratio that was not, and the paper supplies no
       number that a chemist could test against a new measurement.
    -- no selection of the wall value from data.  Section 5.3.
    -- no proof of the knot converse.  Section 6.
    -- no claim that the twin-prime leg is part of the peel theory.  It shares the ledger
       language and nothing else, and it is included as the same author's ledger on a
       different object.
    -- no claim that theta is formalised.  Only the algebraic step from oddness to vanishing
       is.
    -- no claim that these developments were originally one programme.  They were not; they were
       separate papers, and the unification is retrospective.

11.  HOW A READER CHECKS IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One command builds the whole Lean body; one builds the audit; one reproduces the archive
census; one reproduces the fraction checks.

    python3 scripts/build_unified.py            # 95 modules
    python3 scripts/build_unified.py --count    # the declaration census
    LEAN_PATH=.:unified:$LEAN_PATH lean unified/Bridge/Audit.lean
    LEAN_PATH=.:unified:$LEAN_PATH lean unified/AuditAll.lean
    python3 scripts/atlas_census.py
    python3 scripts/shapes_recall.py
    python3 scripts/knot_census.py
    python3 scripts/ramanujan_faces.py
    python3 scripts/check_paper_numbers.py

The build covers 95 modules and exits zero -- 96 discovered including the unified root, less
UnitaryEntropy, which needs Physlib and is excluded.  The census counts 1,631 theorem and
lemma declarations in the six trees, of which 1,628 are in modules the build compiles (the
three in UnitaryEntropy are not); the figure is a parser's, and the parser is named.  Two
audits cover the paper's PROVED citations: Bridge/Audit.lean reports 39 declarations and
AuditAll.lean another 63 -- the common-clock set, the 37 prime-gap declarations, the Smith
identity and the Dirac gap theorem -- every one within [propext, Classical.choice,
Quot.sound] (several use fewer).  The Bridge audit prints 41 physical lines because one
report wraps.  The census, shape, knot and fraction scripts print their totals and pass.  The last
reads this file and checks fifteen selected computed values against it.  That is a tripwire,
not a guarantee: it matches substrings, it does not parse the prose, and a figure it does not
list can drift without it noticing.  Anything that does not reproduce is a bug and should be
reported as one.

12.  OPEN PROBLEMS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    -- The wall value is not selected by the data.  What measurement would select it?  A new
       ordinary-step ratio or anion rung strictly between 5.807902 and 5.866539 would narrow
       the combined classification band; no shipped ratio lies in that open interval.  (The
       older, wider step-only band is not the live one: silver's, silicon's and iridium's
       anion rungs already sit inside it.)  This is the one place where a new measurement,
       not a new argument, could change the paper.
    -- The knot converse is open, and the lemma cannot reach it.
    -- The two constants 4/pi^2 and 1/4 in the fraction error law are observed and not
       derived.  A derivation would turn Section 4.4's measurement into a theorem.
    -- The predictive quotient of a peel is model-dependent.  Whether any canonical choice
       of (L, R) is forced by the peel structure is open, and the negative result in 7.3 is
       the honest state of it.
    -- The physical bridge, for every leg, is untouched.  Nothing in this paper moves it.

A note on how this file is kept

This is the verbal spine, not the paper's final text.  When a claim is verified, its status
changes here and in the ledger; when a claim fails, it moves to Section 7 with the corrected
form beside it.  The repository's file names are used rather than theorem numbers so that
the text survives refactoring.  Numbers quoted without a script are to be removed, not
trusted.
