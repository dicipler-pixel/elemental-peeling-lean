# What reproduces all four properties: the pair (group, period)

I overstated it. There *is* a coordinate that reproduces all four — it is a **pair**, and it is the one
the universal scale already has. What fails is any **scalar**.

## 1. The table

$R^2$ of a linear fit of each property on each model (log $y$ for $IE_1$ and radius; main group, $s$ and
$p$ blocks, $n\approx40$–50):

| model | EN | $IE_1$ | radius | mass | molar heat | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $g$ (group) | 0.670 | 0.319 | 0.113 | 0.058 | 0.116 | 0.255 |
| $m$ (period) | 0.154 | 0.328 | 0.457 | **0.886** | 0.300 | 0.425 |
| $Z$ | 0.046 | 0.141 | 0.265 | **0.999** | 0.180 | — |
| $h$ (countdown) | 0.541 | 0.530 | 0.334 | 0.009 | 0.156 | — |
| **$(g,m)$** | **0.816** | **0.648** | **0.604** | **0.902** | **0.502** | **0.694** |
| $(g,m,k)$ | 0.883 | 0.813 | 0.608 | 0.903 | 0.504 | 0.742 |
| $(g,m,k,h)$ | 0.892 | 0.822 | 0.609 | 0.932 | 0.556 | 0.762 |

where $k$ is the occupancy of the subshell being filled (the element's position inside its pair-slot).

**Read down the columns.** Mass is a function of $Z$ alone (0.999) and of nothing else; EN and $IE_1$ are
functions of the group; radius is mostly the period. **Read across the rows.** No scalar exceeds 0.9 on
one property and fails on the rest: $Z$ gets mass and nothing else (EN 0.046), $h$ gets EN and $IE_1$ and
loses mass entirely (0.009), $m$ is middling everywhere.

## 2. Why a scalar cannot work — the structural reason

$$\text{mass}:\ \text{monotone in } Z;\qquad
\text{EN},\ IE_1,\ \text{radius}:\ \text{periodic in } Z .$$

A scalar is either monotone or periodic. $Z$ is the first, $h$ (or $g$) is the second. The pair is the
smallest object that is both — exactly the two-coordinate split the universal scale used: **$m$ carries
the monotone part (size), $g$ carries the periodic part (valence)**, and since $Z=C(m)-h$ the pair
*contains* $Z$ and therefore contains the mass.

So the correct statement is not "no coordinate fits all four" but: **one coordinate is provably not
enough, and two are.**

## 3. Your double twin / triple twin

You are right in kind: two, not one — and the primes already say so. A segment carries two numbers,
(class, size): a residue-like periodic one and a magnitude one. The element side's pair,
(group, period), has the same character, which is why it works.

What the *triple* does, measured:

| | $(g,m)$ | $(g,m,k)$ | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| EN (main group) | 0.816 | 0.883 | +0.067 |
| $IE_1$ (main group) | 0.648 | **0.813** | +0.165 |
| radius | 0.604 | 0.608 | +0.004 |
| mass | 0.902 | 0.903 | +0.001 |
| heat | 0.502 | 0.504 | +0.002 |

The third coordinate earns its place on the ionisation energy and nowhere else. And inside each block it
adds **nothing** — $s$ 0.558→0.558, $p$ 0.791→0.791, $d$ 0.485→0.485, $f$ 0.444→0.444 — because within a
block the occupancy is a monotone relabelling of position, so it is collinear with what is already there.
Its whole value is telling you *which block* you are in, which the group only half does.

A fourth ($h$, on top of $g$) buys 0.02 on the mean and nothing meaningful. So the ladder of models
closes at two, with one marginal third.

## 4. The answer, in one line

The **pair (group, period)** reproduces all four properties at once (mean $R^2=0.69$ on the main group,
with mass $0.90$ and EN $0.82$); the **triple adds only $IE_1$**; and no single number does it, because
mass is monotone in $Z$ while the other three are periodic in it — one coordinate cannot be both, and two
can.
