# `What the Peel Keeps` v1.2.2 — the ten credit lines, and what the record supports

Corrections for an independent check. The subject is the 72-page PDF
`What_the_Peel_Keeps_Codex_Working_Review.pdf` (22 November 2026). Page numbers are the printed
folio. The evidence base and the dated ownership table are in
[`a-page-is-a-record.md`](a-page-is-a-record.md).

The argument is not that the document credits too much or too little in general. It is that **ten
specific lines give an author's credit for material whose dated record is Jeromie Beasley's**, and
that the same document's §9.4 already says so in its own words.

## What stays hers, and should stay

* **The one credit:** the notebook pages and the Mixin colour labelling system (`@Ellyrox110`), named
  together, in §9, where the labels are used.
* **Not a credit line at all:** the heading on page 37, "Questions she asked me in our conversation,
  answered". That reports a conversation and names the questions; it asserts nothing about
  authorship and should be left exactly as it is.
* **A dependency, not a credit:** the blind colour readings for the preregistered test, if and when
  they are supplied.

That is a citation and a thanks. It is not an author line, and it does not extend to the algebra
that translates her vocabulary onto the peel.

**The target is one credit, not ten.** Her name appears ten times in this document, and nine of
those times it is attached to something she did not do. The correction is not to soften ten lines
but to delete nine of them and keep **one**, at the point where her material is actually used —
the Mixin labels in §9, with the notebook pages named in the same sentence. Everything else about
her comes out. A scattered name is not a fuller credit; it is ten claims, each of which she would
have to defend.

## The one structural point

Page 37 already gets this right:

> **Questions she asked me in our conversation, answered.** Layered: yes … Moving in parallel like
> conveyor belts: nothing in the data shows it yet … An inserted charge: yes … "Too close to the
> ceiling": a step that jumps too far …

Four questions, four answers, one heading that separates them. That is what a conversation
contributes: the questions. It is not co-authorship of the answers, and the document says so
correctly here while saying the opposite on pages 1, 35 and 67.

## Why the body and the front matter disagree

§9.4 contains the sentence that settles it:

> Her drum is the difference face and her ten is the sum face: **this is my reflection written in her
> own terms.**

A translation is not authorship of the thing translated. The body knows this; the front matter,
the section opener and the colophon do not. Bringing those three lines into agreement with §9.4 is
the whole of correction 1, 4 and 10.

## One thing not to do

Do not delete her from the document. The Mixin labels and the notebook pages are hers and the paper
is better for using them with the credit attached. The correction is to move her from the author
line to the places where her material actually appears — which is where a source belongs, and where
her contribution is visible instead of being diffused into a byline.
