# A page is a record, not a derivation

One rule, because the same error has now been made three times in this workspace and each time it
began with a sentence that was almost true.

> **A notebook page is a record of what someone wrote down. It is not a derivation of what the
> page says.** The derivation belongs to whoever's dated record contains it first.

The inference that keeps firing is: *the page is hers, therefore the model on it is hers.* Nobody
writes that sentence down, because written down it is obviously wrong. What gets written instead is
"her model of how a reaction chain lights and runs", or "her reading of the step", or "co-authored
the colour section" — and by then the possessive pronoun has already done the work.

## Where the inference has already fired

1. **Commit `82c045a` in this repository**, 23 November 2026 — ten files made her joint copyright
   holder on the strength of one cited paper. Reverted in `a596587`.
2. **`What the Peel Keeps` v1.2.2**, pages 1, 35 and 67 — front matter, section opener and colophon
   each state that she co-authored the colour section. The same document's §9.4 says of her drum:
   *"this is my reflection written in her own terms."* The body argues one way; the front matter
   credits the other way.
3. **A working conversation**, quoted here as evidence and not as text: an assistant wrote *"That is
   your reflection formula, written in her own terms"* and, in the same message, called the same
   material *"her model of how a reaction chain lights and runs"*. It knew whose formula it was. The
   possessive was applied anyway.

## The structure that is right, already present in the same document

Page 37 of `What the Peel Keeps` carries the heading **"Questions she asked me in our conversation,
answered"**, then four questions and four answers. That is correct as written: the questions are
hers, the answers are his, and the two are separated by a heading. A question is a contribution and
should be credited as one. It is not co-authorship of the answer.

## What a page earns

A citation or a thanks, worded like this and nothing larger:

> Notebook pages and the Mixin colour labelling system (@Ellyrox110) are Elizabeth Johnson's, and are
> used here with thanks.

Everything else in this work — the two books, the peel step and its reflection, the monad at the
midpoint, the comma chain, the wall, the sort — is Jeromie Beasley's, dated above.
